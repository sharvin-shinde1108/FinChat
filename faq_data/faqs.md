# Finance FAQs

## What does a high Debt-to-Equity (D/E) ratio mean?
A high D/E ratio indicates that a company is financing a larger portion of its operations with debt relative to shareholder equity. This can amplify returns in good times but increases financial risk and interest burden, especially if cash flows are unstable.

## What is Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio?
P/E = Price per share / Earnings per share. Higher P/E can imply expectations of future growth; lower P/E may indicate undervaluation or slower growth. Always compare to peers and the firm's own history.

## What is Free Cash Flow (FCF)?
FCF is cash generated by operations minus capital expenditures. It indicates how much cash a company can return to shareholders, pay down debt, or reinvest.

## What is Return on Equity (ROE)?
ROE = Net Income / Shareholder Equity. It measures how efficiently equity is used to generate profits. Sustained high ROE without excessive leverage is generally positive.

## What is EV/EBITDA?
Enterprise Value to EBITDA compares enterprise value to operating earnings. It’s useful for comparing companies with different capital structures.

## What is MACD?
Moving Average Convergence Divergence is a trend-following momentum indicator showing the relationship between two EMAs. Crossovers and divergences are commonly analyzed.

## What is RSI?
Relative Strength Index measures the speed and magnitude of price moves. Values over ~70 are often considered overbought; under ~30 oversold (context matters).

## What is VWAP?
Volume Weighted Average Price shows the average price weighted by volume. Traders watch intraday price vs VWAP for mean reversion or trend strength.

## What is a dividend payout ratio?
Payout ratio = Dividends / Net Income. A very high ratio can be a red flag if earnings are volatile.

## What are common risks when rates rise?
Higher rates can compress valuation multiples, raise interest expense, slow consumer demand, and tighten credit conditions.
